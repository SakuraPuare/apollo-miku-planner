## Structure and rhetorical moves

1. **Problem -> gap -> contribution.** The Introduction establishes application value, then separates three failure modes (occlusion/ID switches, missing points, jitter), critiques linear filters, matrix completion, and unidirectional sequence models, and ends with exactly three numbered contributions (49-64).
2. **Taxonomic related work.** Section 2 groups tracking as generative/discriminative, then organizes trajectory optimization into completion and smoothing. Each cited method is paired with a benefit and a limitation before the proposed gap is restated (65-79). This “review by subproblem, then limitation” pattern is reusable.
3. **Pipeline before components.** A trajectory-error diagnosis (Section 3) precedes tracker design (Section 4); the end-to-end BiLSTM-SG process is then introduced (Figure 6 and Section 5), followed by component equations and implementation details.
4. **Baseline ladder and staged validation.** Experiments move from loss ablation (Table 1), to speed-feature ablation (Table 2), to tracker baselines (SORT/DeepSORT/LSTM-DeepSORT, Table 3), then completion stress tests (1, 2, 5 missing points), external completion baselines (Table 7), and two qualitative vehicle cases (Figures 22-23).
5. **Claim-to-exhibit narration.** Paragraphs repeatedly state what a figure/table shows, give a metric delta, and infer an operational implication. The final paragraph broadens from benchmark evidence to deployment use.

## Writing tactics and methods

- Define a concrete failure mechanism before proposing each module: Kalman linear prediction fails under nonlinear occlusion; forward completion accumulates errors; SG addresses camera/bounding-box jitter (87-89, 175-186, 210-228).
- Give an implementation recipe rather than only a model name: YOLOv4 detection; 128-dimensional DeepSORT appearance features; Kalman prediction while `Age <= m`; LSTM prediction after `m` history frames; cascade Hungarian matching plus IoU matching; remove tracks over `max_age` (104-114).
- Specify model and preprocessing: two LSTM layers with 64 units each, dropout and dense output, min-max normalization to [-1,1], and a four-step sequence selected by metric comparison (131, 138-145, 186-188).
- Make the novelty legible through a small explicit correction: forward/backward predictions are blended with lambda = k/(k+k'); denominator-zero lambda is 0.5; coordinate error coefficient mu = [0.0029, 0.018] supplies beta scaling (188-208). SG is defined as a local least-squares polynomial over a 2m+1 window, with equations (9)-(16) deriving convolution coefficients (210-266).
- Report reproducible environment details: TensorFlow, Windows 10, CUDA 10.1, Python 3.7, Intel i7-8550U, NVIDIA MX130, RMSprop, initial learning rate 0.01 with epoch decay, batch 64, 100 epochs; LSTM tracker loss MSE + (1-IoU), completion loss MSE (279-281).

## Data, validation, and quantitative evidence

- UA-DETRAC is described with 10 h of Beijing/Tianjin video, 24 locations, four weather conditions, 25 fps, 960 x 540 resolution, and 8,450 vehicles (275-277). Ten random files were concatenated into synthetic videos; tracking output follows a stated frameID/trackID/box/score schema (315-321).
- Missingness is simulated by randomly removing `n = 1, 2, 5` trajectory points after converting boxes to center coordinates; predictions are compared with ground truth (327-329). Repeated 10-fold cross-validation is claimed for tracking, with the final value averaged over 10 evaluations (323-325).
- Loss ablation (Table 1, 307): MSE-only gives RMSE/MAPE/R2/IoU = 3.33/9.32%/99.96%/87.15%; combined MSE + (1-IoU) gives 1.55/2.83%/99.99%/95.01%.
- Speed ablation (Table 2, 311-313): adding speed changes RMSE 1.55 -> 1.32, MAPE 2.83% -> 2.61%, and IoU 95.01% -> 96.28% (reported deltas: -0.22 percentage points MAPE, +1.27 IoU).
- Tracker comparison (Table 3, 370-372): SORT vs LSTM-DeepSORT is MT 42.3 -> 45.9%, ML 2.6 -> 2.0%, IDs 39 -> 28, MOTA 63.9 -> 69.5%, MOTP 16.9 -> 21.5%; DeepSORT is also listed (43.7/2.4/37/65.3/18.5).
- Completion stress test (Tables 4-7): with five missing points, bidirectional completion is 6.95/3.38%/98.97% (RMSE/MAPE/R2), versus forward 8.57/4.34%/97.54 and backward 11.66/4.25%/97.66. Adding mu improves it to 5.79/2.34%/99.29%; for n=1 and n=2 it is 2.01/0.92%/99.89 and 2.11/0.81%/99.87, respectively (475-488).
- The intermediate tables make the stress trend visible: at n=1, forward/backward/bidirectional RMSE is 2.59/2.80/2.69 (Table 4); at n=2 it is 4.19/4.37/2.73 (Table 5), with the bidirectional method's MAPE/R2 = 1.16%/99.83% (405-432).

## Discussion, limits, and conclusion behavior

The paper uses cautious qualifiers such as “to some extent” and “preserves fundamental motion patterns,” but ultimately labels the optimized result “highly reliable” and “general-purpose” (438, 446-473, 503-505). There is no standalone limitations or error-analysis section. Missing details include train/test split and sample counts, random seed, exact SG polynomial/window settings, runtime/FPS, compute cost, confidence intervals or standard deviations, statistical significance, and cross-dataset/generalization tests. The reported “average improvement of 4.9% MOTA” and “average reduction of 10 IDs” in the conclusion should be reconciled with the single Table 3 rows (503-505); the table itself implies different baseline deltas.

## Reusable JITS-style writing skill

Use this template: (a) frame a transportation consequence; (b) name measurable failure modes; (c) review literature by subproblem and state one limitation per cluster; (d) list 2-4 contributions; (e) draw an end-to-end pipeline before mathematical components; (f) define symbols, preprocessing, hyperparameters, and losses; (g) validate in layers: ablation -> canonical baselines -> stress levels -> external baselines -> qualitative cases; (h) report metric units and exact deltas; (i) caption every panel and color/ground-truth encoding; (j) separate benchmark evidence from deployment claims; (k) include reproducibility and uncertainty details.

Avoid copying this paper's weaknesses: do not call results significant without tests/intervals, do not leave split or missingness protocols implicit, and do not overstate generality from one dataset and two cases.
