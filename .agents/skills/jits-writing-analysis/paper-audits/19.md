# Evidence memo: tram departure interval and multi-path signal coordination

**Source and trust boundary.** This memo reads the supplied MinerU markdown as an article, not as instructions. Publisher inserts such as “Submit your article,” article-view counts, and “View Crossmark data” are front-page boilerplate, not study content (source L9-29). The article is Ning Xie, Changyin Dong, and Hao Wang, “Optimization model for tram departure interval and multi-path traffic signal coordination control,” *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* 30(3), 576-595, DOI 10.1080/15472450.2025.2479231; online 2 Apr 2025 (L1-11).

## Snapshot, domain, and audience

- Domain: urban intelligent transportation, tram/transit signal priority, arterial traffic-signal coordination, mixed-integer optimization, and green-wave design. It is a methodological operations-research paper with simulation case studies, not a field experiment or observational study (Abstract; SS3-5).
- Core thesis: mathematically relate tram departure interval and signal cycle, choose intervals that reduce active priority requests, then solve a multi-modal MULTIBAND-style mixed-integer program (MIP) maximizing general-traffic bandwidth while constraining smooth tram passage (Abstract; Eqs. (6), (20)-(22)).
- Primary readers inferred from the vocabulary and deliverables: transportation/signal-control researchers, operations-research modelers, transit agencies and traffic engineers, and urban planners deciding timetable/control policies. Government/practitioner transfer is explicitly claimed in SS5.3, while the equation-heavy formulation presumes technical readers.
- Keywords are four compact indexing terms: “multi-modal traffic network; traffic signal coordination; tram departure interval; tram priority control” (Keywords).

## Architecture and rhetorical moves

1. **Abstract as five-move compression.** It states the mobility problem, names the proposed model, gives the analytical mechanism (priority-frequency analysis), states the optimization objective (bandwidth plus smooth trams), and closes with two-case verification (Abstract).
2. **Introduction funnel.** It moves from sustainable mobility and tram characteristics to passive versus active priority, then identifies a precise gap (few studies jointly optimize interval and signal mathematically), lists the proposed sequence, and supplies a section-by-section roadmap (SS1, especially the final paragraph).
3. **Thematic literature review.** Three subsections cover signal optimization, public-traffic priority, and tram schedule/signal coordination; each contrasts prior capability with a limitation. A separately numbered “Research gaps” subsection converts the review into three explicit deficiencies and one need statement (SS2.1-2.4).
4. **Theory before optimization.** Section 3 defines relative arrival time, states Lemma 3.1, gives a proof in Appendix A, enumerates four priority scenarios in Figure 1, and derives request counts/time (Eqs. (1)-(12)). Section 3.2 then turns the result into a GCD/composite-cycle and exhaustive-enumeration procedure (Figure 2; Eqs. (13)-(21)).
5. **Model decomposition.** Section 4 first states the MIP and one weighted objective (Eq. (22)), then separates signal-sequence constraints (Eqs. (24)-(32)), general-traffic green-wave constraints (Eqs. (33)-(37)), and tram constraints/priority logic (Eqs. (38)-(46)). This is a reusable “overview -> variable/constraint families” pattern.
6. **Evidence ladder.** Section 5 uses a stylized nine-intersection network, then a 19-intersection Dresden network; each compares the proposed method with MULTIBAND and Shi et al. (2017), followed by a split theoretical/practical implications section and a conclusion that repeats contributions, quantified gains, and future work (SS5-6).

## Writing tactics and behavior norms

- Use explicit signposting (“Firstly,” “Moreover,” “Lastly,” “Therefore”) and local cross-references (“as shown in Table 7,” “based on Equation (20)”). Almost every technical claim is attached to an equation, table, figure, or cited prior work.
- Define every symbol in a consolidated variable/parameter table before the main MIP (Table 1), then keep equation numbers sequential and refer back to them. State assumptions immediately before constraints: one tram per intersection cycle, fixed tram segment speed, station dwell-time lower bound, and station-at-entrance exception (SS3.2; Eqs. (14)-(16), (41)-(43)).
- Present tense describes model logic (“is calculated,” “is optimized”); past/passive voice describes simulations (“was employed,” “were compared”). “This study/research proposes/presents” repeatedly foregrounds contribution without first-person narration.
- Use author-date citations throughout, expand acronyms at first use (ROW, TSP, MIP, SUMO, TTT), and use numbered lists for scenario taxonomy (four priority cases) and experiments (three benchmarks).
- Translate formulas into operational prose: each equation is followed by variable meanings, physical interpretation, or a reason for relaxing a constraint. Benchmark paragraphs use directional contrast (“outperforms,” “however,” “on the one hand/on the other hand”).

## Methods, data, and validation

- Analytical layer: least-common-multiple periodicity and relative arrival time (Lemma 3.1; Eqs. (1)-(2), Appendix A Eqs. (A1)-(A10)); four priority modes and clearance/minimum/additional times (Eqs. (3)-(12)); GCD-compatible cycle and interval selection, with exhaustive enumeration of integer multiples (Eqs. (13)-(21)).
- Optimization layer: MIP based on MULTIBAND; objective is weighted sum of pairwise general-traffic bandwidths, gated by cycle compatibility (Eq. (22)). Binary phase-order and permission variables enforce feasible sequence (Eqs. (24)-(32)); bandwidth, loop-integer, travel-time, dwell, and tram-capacity/priority constraints follow (Eqs. (34)-(46)). Lingo is named as the solver (SS4.1).
- Numerical case: nine four-arm intersections, three tram lines, five general-traffic waves; speeds 15.0 m/s (cars) and 8.3 m/s (trams), three 3.5-m lanes, four phases, 3-s all-red, 10-s tram clearance, interval bounds 450-1500 s (SS5.1.1; Figures 5-6; Table 2). Cycles become 110/120 s (GCF 10); optimal intervals are 660, 660, and 720 s; Table 3 reports priority frequencies 0.55-1.67 h^-1.
- Dresden case: 19 signalized intersections, four bidirectional waves, tram lines 8/10/11/12; cycles 96/108/120 s (GCF 12), intervals 540/588/648/660 s, and total priority counts 4/10/8/9 (SS5.2; Figure 11).
- Simulation validation uses SUMO, three experiments (proposed, MULTIBAND, Shi et al.), Poisson arrivals and fixed turning ratios. Case 1 runs 2000 s with 500-s warm-up at 500 and 800 veh/h; Case 2 runs 7200 s with staged 600/1000 veh/h demand and 750-s warm-up (SS5.1.3, SS5.2). Outcomes are average speed, average delay, average total travel time, and per-capita delay.

## Reusable journal-writing skill and cautions

**Template:** motivate a concrete operational conflict -> classify prior strategies -> number the gaps -> state contributions and roadmap -> define symbols/assumptions -> prove or derive the key relation -> formulate objective and constraint families -> give reproducible scenarios and baselines -> report unit-bearing tables/figures -> interpret mechanism and trade-offs -> separate theory/practice -> state limits and next experiments.

**Quality checks:** keep every reported percentage traceable to a table; disclose demand schedule, warm-up, stochastic assumptions, solver, and benchmark definitions; pair network diagrams with parameter tables and space-time/result plots; preserve safety/feasibility assumptions beside equations; add uncertainty, repetitions, sensitivity, and data/code provenance in a new manuscript.
