# Paper 35 audit: unknown actuator-delay compensation in CACC

- **Title:** “Compensation of unknown large actuator delays in Cooperative Adaptive Cruise Control vehicles via the truncated predictor feedback.” It is a 2026 *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* article (vol. 30, no. 4, pp. 911–923; DOI shown in the source).
- **Domain/type:** a theorem-led control-design paper for connected-vehicle platoons, followed by deterministic simulation and a baseline comparison. It is not an observational traffic study, human-subject study, field experiment, or statistical learning paper. The conclusion calls it a “brief.”
- **Likely primary readers:** CACC/platooning and intelligent-transportation researchers; control and time-delay-system theorists; engineers concerned with string stability and implementable feedback. ## Macrostructure and rhetorical moves

1. **Compact, unstructured abstract:** application problem -> explicit setup (homogeneous CACC, CTH policy, PF topology, third-order delayed dynamics) -> proposed low-gain TPF -> formal claims (individual/string stability and zero steady-state error) -> simulation/baseline result.
2. **Introduction as a narrowing funnel:** motivates safety/throughput; defines individual versus string stability; explains actuator-delay mechanisms and practical large-delay cases; surveys short-delay and predictor families; classifies delays as known/uncertain/unknown; identifies the unmet “completely unknown long actuator delay” case; then states contributions and justifies the PD-CACC benchmark. The final two paragraphs separate theoretical novelty, computational simplification (dropping the history integral), benchmark choice, and sensor-delay extension.
3. **Problem formulation/control design:** derives the model rather than presenting a controller abruptly. Equations (1)–(8) move from third-order vehicle dynamics and CTH spacing to error-state dynamics; Eq. (9) defines the PD baseline; Eqs. (10)–(14) derive the TPF gain from a parametric ARE.
4. **Analysis before experiments:** first derives the baseline’s finite delay margin via Eqs. (16)–(19), then states/proves individual stability (Theorem 3.1), proves $L_2$ and $L_\infty$ string stability (Theorem 3.2; Eqs. (22)–(25)), and only then adds joint actuator/sensor delay through Eq. (26).
5. **Validation as threshold-crossing comparison:** one benign case, one case just beyond the baseline limit, and one stress case. The prose repeatedly maps each plot back to theorem outcomes: oscillation/overshoot, disturbance attenuation, and steady-state spacing error.
6. **Conclusion:** restates contribution and comparison, explains implementation economy, then gives explicit limitations/future work (communication delay; heterogeneous vehicles/distinct actuator delays).

## Control method, theorems, assumptions, and evidence

- **Model:** identical vehicles with position, velocity and acceleration and first-order actuator lag $\tau$ plus input delay $D_a$ (Eq. (1)); constant-time-headway spacing $r+h v_i$ (Eq. (2)); analytically sets $r=L=0$; predecessor-follower information flow (Fig. 1). The transformed command combines predecessor desired acceleration with local correction (Eqs. (6)–(8)).
- **Proposed method:** $\xi_i=F(\gamma)x$, $F=-B^TP(\gamma)$, where $P$ solves the low-gain ARE (Eqs. (10)–(13)); Eq. (14) is a finite-dimensional, delay-value-free state-feedback implementation.
- **Theorem 3.1 assumptions:** $A$ is not exponentially unstable (the paper phrases this as real eigenvalues in the closed left half-plane), $(A,B)$ is controllable, and the positive low-gain parameter $\gamma$ is “tuned appropriately.” The proof uses the Walton–Marshall imaginary-axis crossing construction; the paper also cites a Razumikhin/low-gain Lyapunov result. Baseline gains $k_p=0.2,k_d=0.7$ yield $D_{a,\max}=1.33$ s (Eqs. (16)–(19)).
- **Theorem 3.2:** strict $L_2$ stability is expressed using $H_\infty$ bounds (Eq. (22)); the key cancellation gives $\Gamma_i(s)=1/(hs+1)$ (Eq. (23)), independent of vehicle index and actuator delay. For $L_\infty$, Eq. (25) plus impulse response $h^{-1}e^{-t/h}$ gives $L_1$ norm 1.
- **Important boundary conditions:** the main theorem is for a homogeneous platoon; Remark 1 says heterogeneous $L_2$ string stability requires the restrictive ordering $\tau_i\leq\tau_{i-1}$. The nominal design assumes delay-free V2V delivery of predecessor desired acceleration and available sensed position/speed/acceleration/error. Communication delay is excluded. Sensor delay is an added frequency-domain study, not part of the nominal theorem.
- **Validation:** six vehicles (leader plus five followers), CTH $h=0.2$ s, simulation step 0.01 s, initially at equilibrium, leader acceleration/deceleration maneuver, and $D_a\in\{1,1.5,10\}$ s. Table 1 fixes $\tau=0.3$ s, $k_p=0.2$, $k_d=0.7$, $\gamma=0.05$. The text reports a TPF stability threshold of 12.94 s for that tuning: both methods remain stable at 1 s (Fig. 6), while the baseline fails and TPF remains stable at 1.5 s (Fig. 7) and 10 s (Fig. 8).

## Reusable writing techniques and cautions

- Define each theoretical criterion in physical terms: $L_2$ as energy dissipation and $L_\infty$ as maximum overshoot/safety. This makes formal analysis legible to transportation readers.
- Build novelty by a **taxonomy plus limitation matrix**: small versus large delay; known versus uncertain versus unknown; exact predictor/history integral versus truncated state feedback. Then state precisely what information the new controller removes.
- Choose a credible domain-matched baseline and explain why. Design cases around a derived threshold (1 s below 1.33 s; 1.5 s above it; 10 s near the proposed 12.94 s margin), so every figure tests a prior claim.
- Keep a traceable chain: assumptions -> numbered model -> controller -> theorem conditions -> metric -> figure. Pair every qualitative plot claim with the relevant parameter values and stability definition.
- **Do not copy the strongest wording uncritically.** “Arbitrarily large/regardless of delay” is conditional on choosing $\gamma$ appropriately, while a fixed $\gamma=0.05$ has the finite reported margin 12.94 s. If the delay and its upper bound are both unknown, explain how tuning can guarantee the needed margin. Distinguish delay-independent implementation from delay-independent gain selection.
- Avoid extrapolating “improved performance” beyond what is measured: the paper shows stability/headway advantages in an idealized linear simulation, not field safety, comfort, actuator saturation, computational load, noisy sensing, packet loss, heterogeneous fleets, or communication delay.
- Clean OCR/layout artifacts before imitating format: the extracted Fig. 6 caption precedes some panels, several subfigure captions are displaced, symbols alternate between “c” and $\gamma$, and spacing/typographical errors are frequent. Use the numbered logical structure, not the conversion glitches, as the style evidence.
