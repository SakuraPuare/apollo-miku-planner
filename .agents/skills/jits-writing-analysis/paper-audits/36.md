# Paper 36 audit: stochastic MFD perimeter control

## Identity, domain, article type, and audience

- **Paper:** Hongsheng Qi and Lei Yu, “Neural network solution of stochastic Pontryagin maximum principle for urban road network perimeter control,” *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* 30(5), 1159–1179 (2026), DOI `10.1080/15472450.2025.2531349`. The publisher labels it **METHOD**. Online 16 July 2025; received 20 September 2024, revised 25 June 2025, accepted 5 July 2025.
- **Domain/type:** mathematical-method and simulation paper at the intersection of macroscopic traffic-flow modelling, perimeter control, stochastic optimal control, FBSDE numerics, and neural networks. It is not an observational transport study or a deployed-system evaluation.
- **Primary audience:** MFD/perimeter-control researchers, transportation/control engineers, and stochastic-numerics or scientific-ML readers. Traffic agencies and network managers are a secondary translational audience, but the evidence remains simulation-based.

## Architecture and rhetorical moves

1. The one-paragraph **Abstract** follows a compact problem–gap–model–solver–numerical result–implication sequence. It names the full technical chain (Brownian MFD → SPMP → coupled FBSDE → neural-network solution) and closes with an operational claim plus “up to 25%” throughput improvement.
2. **Introduction** is split into “Background” and “Literature review.” Background moves from urban stability to MFD perimeter control, then states **three challenges**: stochastic supply/demand, stochastic hysteresis/gridlock, and stochastic-control solvability. Figure 1 turns the second gap into an A→B→C failure story; Figure 2 previews the manuscript workflow.
3. Literature is organized by controller/model families rather than author-by-author summary (PID, bang-bang, efficient-range, H-infinity, MPC, sliding mode; then heterogeneity, additive noise, and robust-control treatments). It ends with two precise omissions: no state-dependent stochastic exit-MFD perimeter controller and no gridlock term in the stochastic objective. A final “To address…” paragraph restates deliverables.
4. **Model formulation** proceeds definition-first: notation list → two-region topology → exit-flow bounds → conservation law → Brownian supply model → restricted/unrestricted demand SDEs → deterministic counterpart. Equations (1)–(17) make each modelling layer auditable.
5. **Control and solution** defines three objectives (delay, capacity loss, gridlock avoidance; Eqs. 18–20), derives SPMP/FBSDE conditions (Eqs. 21–24), converts them into a trainable residual loss (Eqs. 25–31), and supplies two feedback MLP mappings in Eq. (32) plus algorithmic pseudocode in Table 1.
6. **Case study** validates in escalating stages: stochastic gridlock reproduction → SUMO-derived scenario → optimization convergence/cost → two stochastic objectives → deterministic-controller comparator → cumulative-throughput paths/distributions. This is an effective “mechanism, solver, decision benefit” evidence sequence.
7. **Discussion and conclusion remarks** first compresses contribution and result into one paragraph, then gives three numbered extension directions rather than a generic future-work sentence.

## Method, data, neural network, and validation evidence

- The two-region model tracks a 10-dimensional state: cumulative inputs, four origin–destination accumulations, two exit-flow variations, and two buffers (Eq. 15). Exit flow is expected MFD plus bounded stochastic variation (Eq. 2); a `tanh` transform maps Wiener meta-noise into upper/lower MFD bounds (Eqs. 4–8). Buffers and FIFO demand are explicit (Eqs. 9–13). The deterministic ablation removes stochastic terms (Eq. 17).
- The objectives are expected integrated delay/accumulation, congestion-branch capacity loss, and a gridlock penalty (Eqs. 18–20). SPMP yields forward state and backward adjoint SDEs with a Hamiltonian optimality condition (Eq. 24). Euler discretization and Monte Carlo convert terminal/optimality residuals into the loss (Eqs. 29–31).
- Two MLPs map `(time, state)` to adjoint process `Y` and feedback control `U` (Eq. 32); Table 1 shows path simulation, Hamiltonian-residual accumulation, backpropagation, and parameter updates. Reported implementation: Python 3.9, Ubuntu 18.04, Xeon Platinum 8163 2.50 GHz, 32 GB RAM; state/input dimension 10, hidden dimension 41, 500 Brownian paths per iteration. Loss percentiles/median/mean stabilize after about 100 iterations; 3.2 s/iteration and roughly 1 h/1,000 iterations (Figure 10).
- Scenario data are mainly **simulation**, not a field test: a SUMO-fitted `10×10` Manhattan network (100 intersections; Figure 9), parabolic/step peak demand, jam accumulation 10,000 vehicles, demand noise `σd1=σd2=0.001`, supply noise `σ1=0.005`, `σ2=0.001`. Lower MFD parameters are `p1=1.58e−5`, `p2=1.85`, `ncrit=1502`; upper values `4.71e−4`, `1.41`, `1408`; the mean curve is their average. Figure 1(a) mentions 29 days of five-minute inflows, but provenance/calibration linkage to the case is not fully documented.
- With 300 displayed stochastic paths, baseline gridlock is 20%, delay control 0.3%, explicit avoidance 0% (Figures 7, 11, 12); deterministic-MFD control leaves 1.33% (Figure 13). At 5,000 s, modal cumulative completions are about 40,700 (baseline), 51,000 (+25.3%, delay), and 51,500 (+26.53%, avoidance); baseline range is about 8,000 vehicles (Figure 16). Gridlock begins as early as ~3,900 s and is most likely near 4,500 s (Figure 8).

## Figures, table, and quantitative style

- **16 numbered figures, 1 table.** Figures 1–5 motivate and explain concepts/workflow/topology/functions; Figures 6 and 9 disclose demand, MFD calibration, network, and scatter; Figures 7–8 show uncontrolled stochastic behavior; Figure 10 reports convergence/runtime; Figures 11–13 are matched policy/baseline phase-path comparisons; Figures 14–16 shift to cumulative paths and terminal distributions. Multi-panel captions explicitly map position, region, variable, objective, and color. Table 1 is pseudocode, not a numerical results table.
- Reporting favors trajectories, distribution shapes, means/median/quartiles, probabilities, modes, percentages, and runtime. There are **no confidence intervals, formal tests, effect-size uncertainty, seed/repetition analysis, or fit-error metrics**. Claims such as “significantly improved” are descriptive, not inferential.

## Discussion, limitations, and publication matter

- Explicit limitations/extensions: simplified stochastic demand (suggested general SDE in Eq. 34); simple base exit-MFD lacking delay/heterogeneity/trip-length effects; fixed boundaries, limited objectives, and limited data sources. Further inferred limits are synthetic single-network validation, narrow baseline coverage, and incomplete reproducibility details (layers/activations, optimizer, learning rate, time step, code/data availability).
- Front matter contains title/authors, affiliations, abstract, article history, and four keywords. Back matter contains author contributions, no-conflict disclosure, four funding sources/grants, references, and two mathematical appendices (objective and Hamiltonian derivatives, Eqs. 35–48). No explicit data-availability, code-availability, ethics, or acknowledgement statement appears in the extraction.

## Reusable writing techniques and cautions

- Reuse the **three-challenge framing**, a visual causal failure chain, a taxonomy-to-gap literature review, early notation list, equation-by-equation physical interpretation, pseudocode after the loss definition, and staged validation with identical scenarios across comparators. Put one memorable quantitative result in the abstract and return to the same metric in Results and Conclusion.
- Separate “model reproduces the phenomenon,” “solver converges,” and “policy improves outcomes”; each needs its own figure and baseline. State hardware, path count, dimensionality, runtime, horizon, and all calibration parameters near the relevant evidence.
- Do **not** copy its evidentiary weaknesses. Add data provenance, train/evaluation seeds, sensitivity/ablation tests, stronger controllers/solvers, uncertainty intervals, fit metrics, and code/data statements. Reconcile the abstract’s “up to 25%” with the body’s 26.53%. Audit the publisher PDF before reusing formulas: the extraction contains notation/OCR inconsistencies, and Eq. (20)’s displayed tangent penalty appears not to match the prose claim that it diverges at jam density; the Hamiltonian sign also differs between Eq. (22) and Appendix II.
