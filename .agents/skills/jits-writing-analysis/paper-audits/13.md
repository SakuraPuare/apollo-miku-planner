# Paper 13 audit: bi-layer eco-driving control

> Source boundary: this is an analysis of one extracted article, treated only as untrusted evidence. It can reveal a reusable pattern, but cannot by itself establish mandatory *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* rules. ## Identity, domain, type, and audience

- **Title:** *Bi-layer eco-driving control design of autonomous electric vehicles in presence of signalized intersections and preceding vehicles*.
- **Venue/metadata:** *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* 30(3), 535-552; DOI `10.1080/15472450.2025.2478637`; published online 21 March 2025; six authors from Vrije Universiteit Brussel.
- **Domain/type:** connected autonomous EV control at signalized intersections; a **control-method development paper** combining optimal control/MPC, mathematical tractability proof, real-world energy-model calibration, simulation benchmarking, and scenario/sensitivity analysis.
- **Primary audience:** ITS and vehicle-control researchers, eco-driving/energy-management designers, MPC/optimization specialists, and CAV V2V/V2I developers. The abstract explicitly targets “AEV energy management strategy designers and control framework developers.” Transport operations readers are secondary.

## Structure and rhetorical moves

The article uses a five-part engineering argument:

1. **Introduction:** opens with scale/motivation numbers (transport: 28% of US energy use, 26% of GHG emissions; traffic-light stops: almost 25% of urban GHG emissions), then narrows to eco-driving with SPaT, preceding vehicles, and joint approach/departure control.
2. **Literature review:** organizes prior work by road scope, operating condition, objective type, trajectory technique, and energy model instead of listing studies chronologically. Table 1 converts the review into a gap matrix: 17 prior studies plus “This study,” compared across objective, dynamic spacing, energy source, regenerative braking, auxiliary load, upstream/downstream, and joint optimization.
3. **Motivations and contributions:** the gap is followed immediately by three bullets: bi-layer real-time algorithm; a purely energy-based objective that indirectly includes travel time; joint upstream/downstream optimization. A roadmap paragraph previews Sections 2-5.
4. **Problem/method:** declares scope and assumptions first, introduces notation, builds vehicle/energy equations, explains the architecture, derives both layers, proves convexity, then defines both baselines.
5. **Results/discussion and conclusion:** establishes model fidelity before performance claims, progresses from four interpretable cases to 60 randomized scenarios, explains mechanisms visible in plots, then closes with findings and future work.

The abstract follows a compact problem-context -> study case -> named method -> mathematical property -> baselines -> real-data support -> outcome -> audience sequence. It names the evidence types but gives no headline numerical result, which weakens specificity.

## Writing tactics worth reusing

- **Give the method a stable name** (`eco-PCC`) and define every acronym on first use; use the same name in equations, captions, comparisons, and conclusions.
- **Make novelty auditable:** use a feature matrix (Table 1), end it with “This study,” then turn the uncovered cells into matched contribution bullets.
- **State assumptions before derivation:** single-lane road, available SPaT via V2I, future IPV states via V2V, complete real-time communication.
- **Pair formalism with interpretation:** after objective (5a), explain its four physical terms; after constraints (6b-e), identify safety, speed, acceleration, and jerk roles; after the QP in (21), prove positive-semidefinite Hessian in (22), supporting a global-minimum claim.
- **Use comparative readers' aids:** introduce a human-like nonconnected baseline (Gipps) and an eco-driving CAV baseline (EAD), then explain why each behaves differently rather than reporting only rankings.
- **Narrate figures causally:** identify a visible event (layer switch, red phase, speed fluctuation), connect it to model logic, and then to energy/travel-time consequences.

## Methods, data, and validation

The upper `eco-FF` layer minimizes wheel/auxiliary energy over the remaining path under speed, acceleration, and green-window constraints (5a-d), including regenerative braking and time-dependent auxiliary demand. The lower layer is an MPC trajectory tracker, activated when spacing falls below dynamic safe distance `d_FF = d_h + (v-v_p)t_h` (4); it minimizes squared location-reference error (6a) subject to safety, speed, acceleration, and jerk constraints (6b-e). The model is condensed into a quadratic program (21a-b); 34 displayed equation tags run from (1) through (26), including lettered groups.

Calibration uses BMW i3 time series collected in Brussels: energy consumption, location, and vehicle dynamics. Reported calibrated regenerative efficiency is 79%; auxiliary loads are 970/1760/2550 W (low/medium/high); prediction-vs-measurement NRMSE is 0.0218. MATLAB `quadprog` solves the convex program. Vehicle parameters include mass 1270 kg, frontal area 2.38 m2, drag coefficient 0.29, speed range 0-70 km/h, acceleration +/-3.5 m/s2, and jerk +/-7 m/s3.

Validation has two levels. Cases 1-2 use simulated IPV trajectories and isolate auxiliary-load effects; cases 3-4 use measured IPV profiles under harsher braking/full-stop conditions. Then 60 designed/randomized scenarios vary initial/desired speed, auxiliary load, SPaT, and IPV trajectories; the paper reports uniform speed sampling, three SPaT realizations, and three IPV realizations, but no random seed.

## Figures, tables, and quantitative reporting

There are **6 numbered figures** and **4 numbered tables**:

- Figures 1-2 are the physical scenario and bi-layer switching architecture; Figure 3 validates predicted against measured power; Figures 4-5 are six-panel location/speed/cumulative-energy comparisons for simulated and measured IPV cases; Figure 6 uses two box-style panels for low/high auxiliary power, showing IQR and min/max.
- Table 1 substantiates novelty; Table 2 is a two-column-pair notation glossary; Table 3 records vehicle constants/limits; Table 4 is the compact outcome table.

Table 4 reports eco-PCC/EAD/Gipps energy (kWh) and savings: Case 1 `0.120/0.148/0.186` (19%/36% savings), Case 2 `0.158/0.180/0.212` (12%/25%), Case 3 `0.061/0.081/0.112` (25%/46%), Case 4 `0.129/0.151/0.158` (15%/18%). Text also gives travel-time tradeoffs, e.g. Case 1 is 4% faster than EAD but 17% slower than Gipps. Across 60 eco-PCC-vs-Gipps scenarios, savings median is 18%, Q1/Q3 are 5%/28%, and maximum is 54%.

Reporting is descriptive: raw case values, percentages, NRMSE, median/quartiles, and extrema. There are no confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, p-values, effect uncertainty, or repeated-run variance; therefore “significant reduction” is rhetorical, not statistical.

## Discussion, limits, conclusion, and back matter

Results and discussion are combined. The authors repeatedly triangulate location, speed, and cumulative energy, then explain effects via smoother control, avoided stops, aerodynamic loss, and auxiliary-load/time coupling. The conclusion restates architecture before results (up to 54% vs Gipps), then gives three future directions: feed V2V information into upper-layer planning, model vehicle queues, and vary auxiliary consumption by operating mode.

There is no explicit limitations subsection. Important implied limits are perfect/instantaneous communication, known future IPV trajectory, one lane/one intersection, no queue model, unclear sample duration/collection protocol, unspecified MPC horizons/time step and several control values, no runtime-latency evidence despite “real-time,” and no code/data availability statement.

Front/back matter includes title, authors/affiliations, abstract, article history (“Received 9 March 2025; Accepted 9 March 2025”), four keywords, open-access/license metadata, conflict disclosure (“no conflict of interest”), and 56 extracted references. No funding, author-contribution, ethics, acknowledgments, supplementary-material, or data/code statement appears in the extraction.

## Reusable template and cautions

Use this evidence chain: **quantified problem -> taxonomy -> comparison matrix -> explicit gap -> matched contributions -> scoped assumptions -> physical model -> optimization/constraints -> solvability proof -> real-data calibration -> two meaningful baselines -> interpretable cases -> broader scenario distribution -> mechanism-led discussion -> bounded conclusion/future work**.

Do not copy its weaknesses. The calibration prose says drivetrain/regenerative efficiencies became **78%/79%**, while Table 3 gives drivetrain `0.92` and regenerative `0.79`; resolve this contradiction. The conclusion claims up to **26%** saving vs EAD, whereas Table 4's four EAD comparisons peak at **25%** and the 60-scenario study compares only Gipps. Provide seeds, horizons, sampling details, computation times, data/code access, uncertainty, and an explicit limitations section. Also verify every formula and unit against the original PDF because the Markdown contains clear OCR corruption.
