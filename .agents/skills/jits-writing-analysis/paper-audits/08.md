## Identity, domain, and likely audience

- Journal article in intelligent transportation / traffic-signal operations; DOI and issue metadata identify *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems*, 30(5), 988-1003, online 21 July 2025 (L1-11). - The problem is adaptive arterial signal coordination: dynamically choose the green-start offset so a platoon traverses adjacent and multiple intersections with fewer stops/delay. The claimed novelty is explicit integration of five factors: upstream delay, speed variation, interintersection distance, downstream queue count, and queue-dissipation pattern (L39, L55-59).

## Structure and rhetorical moves

1. **Front matter and compressed abstract.** Author affiliations, one-paragraph ABSTRACT, article history (received 6 Nov 2024; revised 29 Jun 2025; accepted 5 Jul 2025), and four keywords establish provenance and indexing (L31-45). The abstract follows problem -> five-factor model -> Webster integration -> MATLAB/VISSIM test -> indicators -> claim (L37-39).
2. **Introduction as funnel.** It opens with the urban-traffic motivation, then reviews conventional coordination and newer multi-agent/deep-RL/ABC work in two literature clusters (L47-53), identifies the missing dynamic-offset treatment, and states a solution and contribution (L55-59). A standalone “Our contribution” subsection repeats the novelty in one paragraph.
3. **Mechanism decomposition before optimization.** The vehicle trip is divided into idling/startup delay, acceleration, and constant-speed/dissipation stages (L61-71). This gives each of the five factors a causal place before equations are introduced.
4. **Assumptions, then equation chain.** “Model hypothesis” lists five idealizations (homogeneous traffic and common vehicle length/spacing; common post-acceleration speed; signal-compliant pedestrians; no downstream delay/capacity overflow) and briefly acknowledges cross-direction delay and real-world adjustment (L73-89). Subsections proceed from startup delay (Eqs. 1-3), acceleration and cut-in correction (4-6), dissipation point/travel (7-13), adjacent/bidirectional optimization (14-21), multi-intersection extension (22-28), and adaptive volume/cycle/green allocation (29-37) (L93-367).
5. **Applied test and comparison.** Simulation setup, site/period, timing examples, and results are separate headings (L369-413). Results compare current fixed-time, proposed dynamic, and an “advanced” multidimensional artificial-bee-colony scheme, then interpret a delay-versus-stop-rate tradeoff (L407-435).
6. **Conclusion and compliance matter.** The conclusion restates mechanism, site, and benefit, then names adaptive cycle length as future work (L477-479). Acknowledgements, AI-use declaration, disclosure, funding, and references follow (L481-530).

## Methods, data, and validation

- **Modeling technique.** Traffic-wave and Webster delay theory derive queue startup delay; acceleration is position-dependent, with vehicles after position `b` assigned `a_b` (Eq. 4, L117-133). A stochastic cut-in correction uses inverse-normal CDF, risk probability, spacing variance, cut-in frequency, and spacing reduction (Eq. 6, L135-141). A downstream “dissipation point” defines when the arriving platoon need not decelerate (L143-186). The adjacent-intersection objective minimizes summed startup, modified acceleration, and uniform travel time (Eq. 14, L211-243); the bidirectional constraint is `O_(U,D)+O_(D,U)=tau*C` (Eq. 21, L245-251). Multi-intersection offsets are presented as repeated neighboring models (L253-285).
- **Adaptive timing.** Previous-cycle video counts are converted to hourly volume by `3600/C_(g-1)` (Eq. 29, L293-305). Webster computes cycle length and phase green allocation (Eqs. 31-34), then an HJB-inspired correction adds `S/(2mu) z(X)^T P z(X)` (Eq. 35); state `X=[upstream count, queue length, platoon speed]`, basis `z(X)=[1,n_u,Q,V,n_uQ]`, and matrix `P` are said to come from offline simulation training, with no training protocol (L335-351).
- **Simulation.** MATLAB solves the model through a COM interface to VISSIM; traffic information is acquired in real time and offsets/timing are updated by cycle (L369-371). The test has four intersections on Huangpu Road, Dalian, using winter 07:00-08:00 observations split into four 15-minute periods (L373-377). Table 1 gives adjacent distances 566, 188, and 659 m and current coordinated-direction green ratios 67%, 70%, 75%, and 74% (L386-388). The paper says each arterial scheme is simulated for one hour and reports four intersections and both directions (L407-409).
- **Validation limits.** There are no reported replications, random seeds, calibration errors, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, vehicle counts, or sensitivity analyses. The abstract names average queue length and average delay (L39), whereas methods/results and Tables 6-7 use average delay and stop rate (L371, L463-469); queue length is not tabulated. “Significant” is rhetorical, without statistical evidence.

## Numerical, stylistic, and format conventions

- Equations are centered and sequentially numbered (1)-(37), followed immediately by prose definitions of symbols and constraints. Piecewise functions and explicit index domains are favored (L97-115, L215-240).
- In-text citations use author-year parentheses; the 17-reference list is alphabetized by first author and usually includes DOI URLs (L497-530). Tables put units in headers (`m`, `s`, `s/veh`); narrative percentages use two decimals, table delays one decimal, stop rates two decimals.
- The prose repeatedly uses declarative “This paper/study...” sentences, explicit “gap -> propose -> evaluate” transitions, and strong verbs (“establishes,” “ensures,” “significantly improves”). This is efficient for applied engineering writing, but claims should be calibrated to evidence.
- Front/back matter includes grants, generative-AI language-editing disclosure, no-conflict statement, and duplicate funding section (L481-495). No data/code availability or human-subject statement appears.

## Discussion, limits, and reusable skill

There is no dedicated Discussion heading. One results paragraph interprets the comparator tradeoff: the proposed method is slightly worse for delay than ABC but better for stop rate, calling the objectives incompatible (L433). The conclusion claims real-time and congestion benefits, while acknowledging the need to combine dynamic offsets with adaptive cycle lengths (L477-479). Reusable sequence: (a) motivate an operational problem; (b) cluster prior methods and name one precise gap; (c) decompose the mechanism into stages; (d) state assumptions before equations; (e) define symbols and constraints locally; (f) connect the model to an implementable controller; (g) compare baseline, proposed, and strong comparator at local and aggregate levels; (h) put timing/data matrices in tables and causal workflow in figures; (i) report absolute values plus defined relative changes; (j) end with bounded claims and a concrete next experiment.

## Cautions before reuse

Treat this as one example, not a universal Taylor & Francis rule. The extraction contains title spelling inconsistency (“diference” versus “difference,” L1-31), missing/reordered Table 5, broken symbols in Eq. 6 and some variable definitions, and likely superscript loss; verify equations and captions against the PDF. Do not claim queue-length validation unless it is actually reported. Add replication/calibration details, uncertainty, data provenance, comparator implementation, and sensitivity to the five factors. Avoid saying the proposed method dominates: Tables 6-7 show the advanced method often has lower delay, while the proposed method often has lower stop rate.
