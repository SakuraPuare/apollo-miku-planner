# Paper 20 audit: adjacent time-domain timetable resilience

## Identity, domain, type, and audience

- **Title:** *Resilience optimization theory for adjacent time-domain timetables in urban rail transit: a spatiotemporal resource coupling framework based on FSAE-TTD algorithm*.
- **Venue/type:** *Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems* 30(5), pp. 1060-1085, DOI 10.1080/15472450.2025.2538836; full-length methodological research article (published online 1 Sep 2025), not a review.
- **Domain:** urban-rail operations research: transition-period timetabling, rolling-stock circulation, turnaround/depot resource conflicts, and a tailored heuristic.
- **Primary audience:** transport-optimization researchers, metro timetable/rolling-stock planners, operations-control engineers, and decision-support developers. Secondary readers are transit managers seeking operational-cost reductions; the mathematics and notation make it unsuitable as a general-public article.

## Architecture and rhetorical moves

1. **Front-load the complete claim.** The one-paragraph Abstract follows problem -> integrated model -> FSAE-TTD solver -> Beijing case -> practical/generalizability implication. The Introduction first establishes demand heterogeneity with operator-recorded, five-workday 2024 passenger flow (Fig. 1), then identifies three practical gaps, states three numbered contributions, gives headline effects (9.8%, 3.3%, 24.4%), and closes with a section roadmap.
2. **Turn the review into a warrant for the model.** Section 2 separates periodic (Sec. 2.1) from non-periodic work (Sec. 2.2). Citations are compact objective/model/solver summaries, followed by explicit scope limits. A numbered synthesis of two deficiencies and three “research necessity” claims (Sec. 2.3) maps directly onto the contributions.
3. **Explain operations before algebra.** Section 3 moves from a small transition example (Fig. 2), to a space-time network (Fig. 3), five permissible path actions (Sec. 3.2), six conflict classes (Sec. 3.3), and a generalized multi-route/multi-depot layout (Fig. 4). Assumptions are stated as “Characteristics”: non-loop line, no overtaking, fixed run/dwell times.
4. **Formalize by resource family.** Tables 1-3 define sets/indices, parameters, and decisions. Equations (1)-(17) establish train timing and connections; (18)-(21) station arrival/departure headways; (22)-(29) turnaround capacity/conflicts; (30)-(33) depot headways; (34)-(38) cross-resource conflicts; Eq. (39) minimizes section mileage as a proxy for operating cost.
5. **Make the solver traceable.** Section 5 proceeds through data preprocessing, construction of five feasible path-set classes, sequential connection decisions, and the four-stage flowchart in Fig. 15. The prose repeatedly states why domain-informed priority/topological ordering should reduce infeasible random search.
6. **Validate from concrete to transferable.** Section 6 reports hardware/software, real line parameters, adjacent timetables, baseline settings, aggregate and route-level results, then shows applications to Harbin and Nanning. Section 7 restates the problem-method-result chain before explicit limits and future work.

## Writing techniques worth reusing

- Use **paired representations**: verbal operational rule -> infrastructure/process diagram -> mathematical constraint -> symbol explanation. Figs. 6-12 and Eqs. (22)-(38) are the clearest examples.
- Build taxonomies around engineering reality: pre- versus post-platform turnaround, single versus double crossover, forward versus reverse depot movement, and same- versus opposite-direction headways. This makes claimed generality inspectable rather than merely adjectival.
- Use strong signposting (“This chapter… first… second… finally”), numbered contributions, numbered operational strategies, and local summaries. Each method subsection opens with the function/safety rationale before details.
- Report not just an aggregate win but a **mechanism-level explanation**. Section 6.3 connects route changes in Table 10 to depot utilization and explains why GA selects four more long JMQ-CPXSK return paths.
- Keep the conclusion numerically anchored: 20 stations, two depots, 45 input trips, 9.8% versus actual operations, 3.3% cost and 24.4% time improvements versus GA.

## Methods, data, and validation evidence

- Inputs are adjacent stable-period timetables and rolling-stock connections; outputs jointly cover transition timetables, turnaround links, and depot entry/exit plans. The model fixes interstation running and dwell times for signaling/dispatch executability (Secs. 3.4, 4.2.1).
- Changping parameters are said to come from actual operational data (Tables 4-8): minimum safety interval 90 s, maximum headway 600 s, dwell range 25-60 s, 20 stations, four turnaround stations, and two depots. Table 9 gives 45 peak-domain versus 18 off-peak-domain trains, implying 18 turnaround connections and 27 depot returns.
- Implementation: C#, Intel i7-8550 1.8 GHz, 16 GB RAM. GA uses crossover 0.8, mutation 0.08, and stops after 50 non-improving iterations after feasibility; FSAE-TTD uses sigma values 150, 600, 0.1, and 15.
- Table 10 reports total distance 3,136,676 m (actual), 2,925,226 m (GA), and 2,829,378 m (FSAE-TTD); runtime is 3,653 s versus 2,763 s. These raw values support the reported reductions of about 9.8%, 3.3%, and 24.4%.
- Harbin Line 2 (19 stations, one depot, mixed pre/post-platform double turnarounds) and Nanning Line 1 (25 stations, two depots, single/double post-platform turnarounds) provide topology-transfer demonstrations (Figs. 18-19), but no comparative metrics.

## Discussion, limitations, and publication conventions

- There is no separate Discussion; interpretation is embedded in Sec. 6.3. The conclusion explicitly limits applicability to non-loop lines without overtaking and proposes express-service and loop-line extensions.
- Front matter includes authors, one institutional affiliation, Abstract, five Keywords, and received/revised/accepted history. Back matter contains a no-conflict Disclosure and 25 author-date references with DOI links. No funding, acknowledgments, author-contribution, data-availability, or code-availability statement appears in this extracted text.

## Cautions for a reusable journal-writing skill

- Do not equate evidence of lower **section mileage** with broad “resilience,” service quality, or total monetary operating cost without defining and measuring those constructs. Passenger outcomes and disturbance experiments are absent.
- FSAE-TTD is compared numerically only with GA; ALNS/VNS are discussed but not run. Report multiple seeds, dispersion, common stopping/budget rules, sensitivity/ablation, and stronger baselines in a new submission.
- Cross-city claims are visually demonstrated but not quantified. Add the same metrics and feasibility checks for every transfer case, plus data/code provenance and reproducibility artifacts.
- Check internal consistency: Sec. 6.3 says 49 actual up-direction trips, whereas Table 10 totals 52. Also distinguish metres in Table 10 from prose references to “train-kilometers.”
- Treat emphatic “for the first time,” “global optimization,” “robustness,” and “successfully applied” as claims requiring direct evidence. Avoid repeating the same novelty list in Introduction, Sec. 2.3, model summary, and Conclusion.
- The MinerU text contains OCR/layout damage (broken symbols, capitalization such as “beijing changping Line,” and Table 5/6 ordering). Learn the logical format from the paper, not these extraction artifacts; verify equations and captions against the typeset PDF before imitation.
