\begin{abstract}
In two-stage path--velocity planning, time-blind obstacle bounds and local passing-side decisions can rule out feasible passages and cause unnecessary stops in dense traffic. We present MIKU, a constraint-generation method that constructs lateral bands from estimated arrival times, selects a sequence of bands across road stations, and maps safe passing windows into bounds for the existing path and speed quadratic programs. For a fixed-section interval model, the widest lateral band can be identified by sorting and scanning obstacle intervals in $O(k\log k)$ time. In 3{,}500 paired structured-roadway simulations spanning seven traffic scenarios, MIKU increased collision-free goal reaching from 62.69\% for an internal time-blind, fixed-side baseline to 77.46\% (paired difference: 14.77 percentage points; 95\% confidence interval: 13.54--15.97). In a separate set of 700 rolling-replanning simulations, success increased from 62.29\% to 72.29\%. Complete-call planning latency had a median of 9.98\,ms and a 99th percentile of 221.80\,ms in the paired benchmark. These findings show that interaction-aware constraint selection improves path--velocity planning under the tested simulation conditions.
\end{abstract}
