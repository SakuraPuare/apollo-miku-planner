# Claim-to-evidence matrix

This internal file controls the claims allowed in the JITS manuscript. Numerical
results are from the frozen commit `29a337ac70f8c36e0a7bfccc2b6c97eb74a3afc2`.

## Research questions

| Research question | Main evidence | Supported conclusion | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1. Does interaction-aware constraint construction improve collision-free goal reaching and traffic progress over path-velocity baselines? | 3,500 paired open-loop numerical scenarios, seven families, seeds 0--499 | Versus B0, success increases from 62.69% to 77.46% (difference 14.77 pp, 95% CI 13.54--15.97, exact McNemar p = 1.94e-133); collision decreases from 2.17% to 1.14% (difference -1.03 pp, 95% CI -1.37 to -0.71); progress increases by 8.93 pp and jerk RMS decreases by 0.120 m/s^3 | Generated straight-road SL scenarios; B0--B2 are matched internal PVD variants, not published-method reproductions |
| RQ2. Which MIKU mechanisms account for the observed effects? | Seven subtractive ablations on the same 3,500 paired scenarios | Removing Top-K spatial homotopy or threat-aware margins reduces overall success by 10.17 and 10.74 pp; removing the temporal homotopy graph reduces delayed-crossing success by 22.8 pp; removing the robust occupancy tube increases prediction-noise collision by 7.4 pp | Several components have small overall effects and are condition-specific; no claim that every component improves every family |
| RQ3. How robust is the method, and what computational or behavioral costs occur? | Prediction-noise subgroup; runtime quantiles; separate 700-scenario rolling-replanning experiment | Under prediction noise, collision decreases by 7.0 pp and success increases by 3.2 pp, but progress decreases by 1.12 pp and jerk RMS increases by 0.041 m/s^3. Overall P50/P95 runtimes are 9.98/55.93 ms, while P99 is 221.80 ms versus 138.88 ms for B0. In rolling replanning, success increases by 10.0 pp (95% CI 7.57--12.57), collision is unchanged, and episode-runtime P95 increases from 827.20 to 1361.51 ms | Do not claim hard real-time performance or a universal safety-margin gain; rolling results are a separate 700-scenario protocol |
| RQ4. Can the constraint interface be connected to an established PVD software stack? | Apollo 11.0 C++ interfaces and three representative Dreamview behavior cases | The spatial and temporal constraints can be passed through the path-boundary and ST-boundary interfaces while retaining the downstream QP structure | Interface and behavior evidence only; not a native Apollo performance benchmark, end-to-end safety proof, hardware-in-the-loop test, or physical-road experiment |

## Supporting analytical and computational checks

| Claim | Evidence | Allowed wording |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum-gap scan solves the fixed-section interval assignment | 4,000 deterministic instances generated by 20 seeds; scan and exhaustive `2^k` enumeration agree to `1e-12` test tolerance | Exact for the defined fixed-section interval model; not a continuous trajectory-level global optimum |
| B3 gives a computational reference | 70 stratified cases; MIKU/B3 success 77.14%/80.00%, difference -2.86 pp (95% CI -11.43--5.71, p = .754); median runtime 10.44/1835.43 ms | Coarse finite-grid beam-search reference; no equivalence or superiority claim |
| Mean clearance is not globally improved | MIKU-B0 mean difference -0.010 m, 95% CI -0.024 to 0.004 | State explicitly that aggregate clearance is statistically unresolved and that success/collision are separate outcome definitions |

## Prohibited claims

- Public-dataset, field-test, road-test, hardware-in-the-loop, or production-deployment validation.
- Native Apollo latency or end-to-end Apollo performance attribution.
- State-of-the-art superiority or a fair reproduction of a recent external planner.
- Continuous global optimality of B3 or of the complete nonconvex planning problem.
- Uniform improvement in clearance, TTC, runtime tails, or every traffic family.
