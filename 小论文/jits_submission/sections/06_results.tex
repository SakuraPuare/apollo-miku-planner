\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Comparative Benchmark Evaluation}
\label{subsec:results_main}

In theorem verification, the $O(k\log k)$ scan and exhaustive enumeration agreed on all 4,000 instances, with maximum center-band-width difference below $10^{-12}$.

For the primary comparative benchmark, Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the 3,500 paired scenarios. MIKU raised collision-free goal reaching from 62.69\% under baseline B0 to 77.46\%, achieving a paired difference of 14.77 percentage points with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of 13.54 to 15.97 and an exact McNemar test result of $p=1.94\times10^{-133}$. The collision rate fell from 2.17\% to 1.14\%, corresponding to a reduction of 1.03 percentage points with a 95\% confidence interval from $-1.37$ to $-0.71$ and $p=2.91\times10^{-11}$. Mean progress rose by 8.93 percentage points, jerk RMS fell by 0.120~m/s$^3$, and penalized travel time decreased by 0.81~s. The aggregate mean-clearance difference was $-0.010$~m with a 95\% confidence interval of $[-0.024,0.004]$~m.

Examining family-level effects, Panel~(a) of Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_dashboard} locates the difference: the largest success gain occurs in narrow multi-obstacle traffic with an increase of 65.4 percentage points, followed by delayed crossing with an increase of 26.8 percentage points. Crossing-pedestrian and vehicle cut-in families show no aggregate difference because their geometry admits a single dominant passage on either side; MIKU's group-level partition and temporal homotopy do not alter the constraint interface in these single-corridor configurations, confirming that the method introduces no regressions where the baseline interface is already adequate. Prediction noise yields a 3.2-point success gain and 7.0-point collision reduction; however, progress drops 1.12 points and jerk RMS rises 0.041 m/s$^3$, an expected side effect of the conservative uncertainty-expanded occupancy tube when the error bound is large. Panel~d shows that the paired gain comes from converting degraded stops into successful arrivals.

In comparison against the joint spatiotemporal search reference B3 across 70 finite-grid cases, B3 reached 80.0\% and MIKU reached 77.1\%, yielding a paired difference of $-2.86$ percentage points with a 95\% confidence interval of $[-11.43,5.71]$ and $p=0.754$; median runtime was 1,835.43~ms for B3 versus 10.44~ms for MIKU.

\subsection{Component Ablation and Mechanism Attribution}
\label{subsec:results_ablation}

Subtractive ablations on the same 3,500 scenarios use the 5,000-resample bootstrap from Section~\ref{sec:experimental_design}. Removing Top-$K$ spatial homotopy cut success by 10.17 percentage points; removing threat-aware margin, 10.74 points. Removing the temporal homotopy graph cut delayed-crossing success from 89.6\% to 66.8\%, which represents a 22.8-point loss in that family. Removing the robust occupancy tube raised prediction-noise collision from 3.6\% to 11.0\%. Arrival projection and longitudinal grouping produce smaller aggregate changes with value concentrated in specific configurations. Panel~c of Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_dashboard} summarizes the tabulated effects.

\subsection{Robustness, Latency Distribution, and Rolling Replanning}
\label{subsec:results_robustness}

In the prediction-noise subgroup, alongside the 3.2-point success gain and 7.0-point collision reduction from Section~\ref{subsec:results_main}, MIKU's mean progress is 59.73\% versus 60.85\% and jerk RMS 2.274 versus 2.234 m/s$^3$, consistent with uncertainty-expanded occupancy being conservative when the error bound is large.

Complete-call runtime median and P95 match or beat B0, but the P99 tail does not: MIKU's P99 is 221.80 ms versus B0's 138.88 ms, with upper-tail divergence in Panel~b of Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_dashboard}. B2 is slower at all quantiles because it always runs three coordination iterations.

The rolling-replanning protocol evaluates cumulative closed-loop behavior. Across 700 cases, success rises from 62.29\% to 72.29\%, representing an improvement of 10.0 percentage points with a 95\% confidence interval of 7.57 to 12.57, while collision stays at 1.29\% for both methods, mean progress rises from 82.55\% to 90.25\%, and episode-runtime P95 rises from 827.20 to 1,361.51~ms.

\subsection{Production Software-Interface Integration}
\label{subsec:results_apollo}

The Apollo 11.0 integration feeds generated path and ST constraints directly into the production-grade path-boundary and ST-boundary modules, preserving downstream QP objective functions, kinematic equalities, and convex structures. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:apollo_cases}, three representative Dreamview scenarios spanning dynamic overtaking, narrow-passage traversal, and single-lane diversion validate smooth trajectory execution and consistent obstacle negotiation within the standard 0.5\,s Apollo planning cycle, confirming practical compatibility with deployed autonomous driving platforms.
