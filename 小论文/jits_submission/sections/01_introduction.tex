\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Automated vehicles must make progress through dynamic traffic while maintaining collision avoidance and ride quality. Excessively conservative motion planning is therefore not benign: repeated fallback stops can reduce service reliability, obstruct surrounding traffic, and erode the mobility benefit expected from an intelligent transportation system. Motion-planning surveys consequently treat feasibility, safety, comfort, and computation as coupled operational outcomes rather than interchangeable measures \citep{gonzalez2016,paden2016,schwarting2018planning}.

Path--velocity decomposition (PVD) is attractive for online operation because it separates lateral path generation in station--lateral (SL) coordinates from longitudinal speed optimization in station--time (ST) coordinates \citep{kant1986pvd,werling2010,zhou2021dliaps}. Industrial implementations can then solve two structured quadratic programs (QPs) and expose environmental information through path and ST boundaries \citep{fan2018baidu,zhou2021pathqp,wang2022_jfr}. The separation, however, can discard precisely the information needed in dense dynamic scenes. A path boundary formed from a current snapshot or a union over the prediction horizon cannot distinguish an area occupied now from one that will be free when the ego vehicle arrives. In addition, irreversible obstacle-by-obstacle side choices can raise the lower path bound and lower the upper bound in mutually incompatible ways, even when a jointly selected passage remains open.

Joint space--time search, mixed-integer optimization, and corridor methods can coordinate these decisions, but they commonly replace the original solver, enlarge its state, or repeatedly invoke the path and speed stages \citep{mcnaughton2011lattice,kessler2023,yoon2024stcorridor}. This paper instead asks whether the lost interaction information can be restored at the constraint interface of an existing PVD stack. The proposed Multi-obstacle Interaction-aware Kinematic constraint Unification method (MIKU) constructs time-dependent lateral bands, selects spatial and temporal homotopy classes, and compiles them into the box constraints already accepted by the two QPs.

The study addresses four research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[RQ1.] To what extent does interaction-aware constraint construction improve collision-free goal reaching and traffic progress relative to matched PVD baselines?
  \item[RQ2.] Which spatial, temporal, and uncertainty-handling components account for the observed effects?
  \item[RQ3.] Under which operating conditions do robustness, comfort, or computational costs limit the benefit?
  \item[RQ4.] Can the same constraint interface be connected to an established PVD software stack without changing its downstream QP structure?
\end{enumerate}

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate arrival-time-dependent SL projection and group-level obstacle-side assignment in center-coordinate bounds. A continuous-partition lemma reduces the $2^k$ assignments for $k$ active obstacles to $k+1$ candidates, and an $O(k\log k)$ maximum-gap scan identifies the widest band under the fixed-section interval model.
  \item We connect the spatial decision to speed planning through uncertainty-expanded occupancy tubes, safe passage windows, and a layered temporal homotopy graph. The selected timing conditions remain linear ST box constraints, preserving the objectives, kinematic equalities, convexity, and sparsity pattern of the downstream path and speed QPs.
  \item We evaluate each claim through a matched evidence chain: 4,000 deterministic interval checks, 3,500 paired scenarios with confidence intervals and component ablations, a separate 700-scenario rolling-replanning study, a finite-grid joint reference, and three Apollo 11.0 interface cases. The analysis reports adverse tail latency and condition-specific trade-offs alongside the gains.
\end{enumerate}
