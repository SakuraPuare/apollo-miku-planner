\section{Limitations and future research}
\label{sec:limitations}

While MIKU improves interaction-aware constraint generation in multi-obstacle environments, several boundaries limit the current validity and define concrete next steps.

First, regarding prediction models and road geometry, the present empirical evaluation relies on constant-velocity predictions with bounded noise envelopes along structured roadways. Real-world traffic involves irregular road curvature, topological intersections, and multi-modal behavioral uncertainties from surrounding human drivers. This boundary may overestimate collision-rate reductions when prediction errors become systematic rather than bounded. Future extensions will incorporate calibrated multi-modal trajectory prediction distributions, such as Trajectron++ or MTR models trained on naturalistic driving datasets, and generalize the interval abstraction to curved roads exceeding 0.05\,rad/m curvature.

Second, regarding the validation tier, the current study demonstrates algorithmic effectiveness through large-scale numerical simulation and software-in-the-loop tests within Apollo 11.0. Future work will add hardware-in-the-loop latency, sensor-pipeline delay, and chassis dynamics under adverse weather or degraded pavement, together with closed-track experiments and completeness analyses under changing passing-order permutations.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented MIKU, an interaction-aware constraint reconstruction framework for two-stage path--velocity motion planning in dense, dynamic multi-obstacle traffic. By introducing arrival-time-dependent projection, continuous group-level lateral partitioning, threat-aware safety margins, and a temporal homotopy graph, MIKU systematically restores missing interaction timing and spatial topology to the environmental constraint interfaces while strictly preserving the standard convex quadratic programming formulations of downstream path and speed solvers. Furthermore, the fixed-section maximum-gap theorem guarantees that optimal lateral side assignments can be identified via an $O(k\log k)$ scan, avoiding the exponential combinatorial explosion of traditional multi-obstacle allocation.

Extensive comparative evaluations across 3,500 paired scenarios demonstrated that MIKU improves collision-free goal reaching from 62.7\% to 77.5\%, representing an increase of 14.8 percentage points, and reduces collision rates from 2.2\% to 1.1\% relative to conventional time-blind baselines. The performance gains are particularly prominent in challenging narrow passages with a 65.4 percentage point gain and delayed-crossing geometries with a 26.8 percentage point gain, directly confirming that the reconstructed constraints eliminate artificial deadlocks. Ablation studies verified that group-level lateral coordination and threat-aware margins establish viable spatial corridors, while temporal homotopy graphs resolve critical causal timing. With a median complete-planning runtime of 9.98 ms, MIKU satisfies real-time execution constraints in typical autonomous driving control loops.

Software-in-the-loop integration within Apollo 11.0 demonstrated software-interface compatibility with production autonomous driving architectures across representative dynamic overtaking, narrow passage, and lane diversion scenarios without altering native QP solver formulations. Closed-loop rolling replanning further verified trajectory consistency and multi-cycle robustness under the simulation conditions tested. Future work will extend the framework to calibrated multi-modal trajectory predictions, complex curved road networks, and full-scale vehicle-in-the-loop experimental platforms.
