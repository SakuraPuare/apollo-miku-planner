\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Joint space--time planning}

Joint planners retain space--time coupling via an expanded state. Conformal spatiotemporal lattices coordinate position, velocity, and time \citep{mcnaughton2011lattice}; instantaneous-analysis and SLT formulations couple lateral and longitudinal decisions in dense traffic \citep{yang2021_ia_planner,hu2023_hybrid_astar_slt,hu2025_dp_nmpc,qiao2025stjoint}; mixed-integer formulations encode side choices as discrete variables \citep{kessler2023}. These differ structurally from MIKU's sequential convex path and piecewise-jerk speed optimizations; our finite-grid joint search is a computational reference on the same collision checker.

\subsection{Decomposed and iterative coordination}

PVD planners optimize path then speed \citep{kant1986pvd,werling2010}, with convex smoothing and piecewise-jerk optimization making the structure production-ready \citep{fan2018baidu,zhou2021dliaps,zhang2020pathqp}. Temporal optimization, iterative anchoring, candidate pairing, and risk-aware coordination feed information back between stages \citep{liu2017speed,chen2019em,zhang2024_dp_rcg,han2026_contingency,pathspeedstructured2024}, yet arrival-time updates cannot recover a lateral alternative discarded by an irreversible local side decision.

\subsection{Safe-corridor and homotopy-corridor methods}

Safe-corridor methods make the feasible region explicit before continuous optimization. Spatial flight corridors bridge discrete search and trajectory optimization \citep{liu2017sfc}; driving work extends this to spatiotemporal lane-change or Frenet corridors \citep{yoon2024stcorridor,frenetcorridor2025}. MIKU shares the explicit-feasible-region principle but targets mutually consistent SL and ST bounds without replacing the path and speed QPs.

\subsection{Interaction prediction and safety margins}

Prediction-aware planning targets urban regimes where static footprints under-represent pedestrian and vehicle behavior \citep{yangbo2023ped,jeong2021ml}. Since predictions remain uncertain, MIKU uses nominal arrivals to rank alternatives while bounded position and velocity errors expand the occupancy set for collision checking. Threat-dependent margins allocate lateral space by urgency, overlap, relative motion, object type, and local density; time-to-collision enters as one normalized urgency factor among these terms \citep{minderhoud2001extended}.

Table~\ref{tab:method_classes} summarizes the solver-level distinction: joint and mixed-integer planners coordinate arrival timing and group-level lateral decisions by replacing or enlarging the two-QP interface, safe-corridor methods construct collision-free sets yet alter solver structures or isolate corridors sequentially, while MIKU's arrival-conditioned SL projection, threat-aware group partition, and temporal homotopy graph map coordinated decisions directly into box constraints for the downstream path and speed QPs.
