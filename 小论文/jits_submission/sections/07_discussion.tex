\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence establishes}

The main result is a constraint-interface effect rather than a claim that one optimizer dominates all alternatives. MIKU retains the PVD solver but changes when and how obstacle information reaches it. The largest gains occur in narrow multi-obstacle and delayed-crossing configurations, precisely where a horizon union or an irreversible local side choice removes a feasible alternative. In configurations that are already easy for B0, such as the crossing-pedestrian and vehicle cut-in families, the aggregate success rate is unchanged. This pattern supports the proposed mechanism more directly than a uniform gain across all cases would.

The ablations provide a second level of support. Top-$K$ spatial homotopy and threat-aware margins account for most of the overall success difference, while the temporal homotopy graph matters primarily when both before- and after-obstacle passage are reachable. The robust occupancy tube reduces collisions under prediction noise, but the corresponding progress and jerk costs show that safety-oriented expansion consumes usable space. These trade-offs are consistent with corridor and prediction-aware planning studies that treat feasibility, safety, and comfort as competing operational outcomes \citep{yoon2024stcorridor,yangbo2023ped,pathspeedstructured2024}.

\subsection{Operational implication}

For a PVD-based automated-vehicle stack, the practical design rule is to preserve interaction timing before the path boundary is finalized, then pass the selected temporal semantics to the speed stage. This can reduce unnecessary fallback stops without requiring a new nonlinear or mixed-integer downstream solver. The rule is most relevant to narrow work zones, temporary lane diversions, and mixed traffic in which several obstacles occupy a shared longitudinal component. It should be deployed with a latency monitor and a fallback policy because the upper runtime tail and uncertainty-expanded cases remain material.

\subsection{Relation to alternatives}

The finite-grid reference reaches a slightly higher success rate in the small comparison, but at a median measured runtime roughly two orders of magnitude larger. This comparison illustrates the quality--cost trade-off between an expanded joint search and a compiled PVD interface; it is not evidence that MIKU is globally better. Likewise, the iterative B2 baseline shows that simply repeating the PVD stages is insufficient when the missing information is a discarded lateral class. MIKU's contribution is therefore the representation and transfer of constraints, not an assertion that iteration or joint search is unnecessary in every deployment.

\subsection{Implications for evaluation}

The separation between the 3,500-case numerical protocol, the 700-case rolling protocol, and Apollo interface cases is important for interpreting the results. The first estimates matched scenario-level effects, the second tests accumulation under replanning, and the third checks an engineering insertion point. Combining them into one headline score would overstate external validity. A future evaluation should use public or field-collected trajectories, a matched recent external planner, and native runtime instrumentation under the target hardware budget.
